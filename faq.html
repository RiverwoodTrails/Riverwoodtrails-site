Dues, Assessments & Money

How much you pay, when, what happens if you're late, and where the money goes.

When are HOA dues to be paid?

Dues are due upon receipt of the annual invoice, mailed no later than April. They cover the current calendar year (January–December) and may fluctuate slightly year to year based on association expenses and reserve levels. Current annual dues are $350. See Make a Payment for online and by-mail payment options.

What happens if I don’t pay my dues on time?

A late payment triggers a $10/month late charge, starting the first of the month after the due date, up to a maximum of $120 per unpaid assessment. Unpaid amounts become the homeowner’s personal obligation — including any successor who later buys the home — and can be pursued through collection or a lien on the lot.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Late Charge; Acceleration → Source: Covenants — Article IX, Late Charge & Acceleration →
What is a special assessment, and can the board levy one?

Yes — beyond the regular annual dues, the Board can levy a special assessment against lots to cover an unbudgeted expense, with the approval of a majority of members voting in person or by proxy at a meeting called for that purpose.

Source: Covenants — Article IX, Special Assessments →
What is the Reserve Fund for?

The Reserve Fund is money the Board sets aside from dues to cover major future repairs and replacements — things like common-area landscaping, entrances, and other shared assets — instead of relying on a surprise special assessment when something wears out.

Source: Covenants — Article IX, Reserve Fund →
Can the HOA place a lien on my home over unpaid dues?

Yes. Unpaid assessments and late charges become a continuing lien against the lot. If the total past-due balance (including late charges) exceeds $400, the Board can file a certificate of lien with the county, and the lien stays valid for five years unless it’s paid, released, or discharged by a court.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Liens →
When do I see the HOA’s annual budget?

The Board delivers a summary of the annual operating budget and assessments to every owner at least 30 days before the fiscal year starts, along with the capital budget. If the Board doesn’t adopt a new budget on time, assessments simply continue at the prior year’s budgeted amount until a new one is adopted.

Source: Covenants — Code of Regulations, Budget →

Design Review & Home Improvements

Additions, decks, driveways, landscaping, and the approval process that covers all of it.

What’s the process for getting approval for a home or yard change?

Complete a Design Review Application and submit it to the board — by email or mail — before starting any work. Include the type and nature of the improvement, color, dimensions, estimated cost, location, and a scale drawing showing placement relative to your lot lines and home. The Design Review Board reviews and approves each application.

Source: Design Review Application →
Can I add on to my house or deck?

Yes, but only after the HOA has approved design plans and all permits are obtained from local governments. No decks are allowed to extend past the width of the house, unless it’s a staircase coming down from the deck.

Source: Covenants — Article V, Architectural Standards →
Can I extend the width of my driveway?

Yes, but it must be identical to the construction of your current driveway, with all local codes and permits in order before work proceeds.

Source: Covenants — Article V, Modifications →
Can I make my landscaping different?

Usually yes, but all plans need to be reviewed. Bold or exotic landscape designs aren’t approved — they must blend well with what’s typical in the neighborhood.

Source: Covenants — Article V, Architectural Standards →
What is a “no-build zone,” and can I build or plant there?

Some areas of the neighborhood — shown on the recorded plat or in prior deed restrictions — are designated “No-Build Zones,” where no structures may be built. In vegetated no-build zones you may still do safety-related maintenance, like removing poisonous or hazardous growth; grassed no-build zones must be mowed, trimmed, and watered by whoever is otherwise responsible for that area.

Source: Covenants — Article VI, Easements →
What happens if I make a change without HOA approval first?

Starting exterior or yard work before it’s approved is a real risk — the HOA can require you to change or remove the improvement entirely, at your own expense. It’s always faster and cheaper to submit a Design Review Application first.

Source: New Resident Welcome Guide →

Exterior Colors, Roofing & Siding

What colors and materials are pre-approved for paint, roofing, and siding.

Can I change the color of my roof or siding?

Roof: no. Siding samples, along with trim color samples, must be approved through the HOA, unless it’s for a repair where all colors match completely.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Shingle Color →
Can I paint my trim, door, and shutters a different color?

Yes, but the color must be pre-approved, and bold or exotic colors are not accepted.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Doors and Shutters →
What siding material or brand am I supposed to use if I replace mine?

Siding should be similar in style and quality to Norandex Summit Manor Dutch Lap Vinyl, 4–5 inches in size, in white, silver/light grey, cream, tan, almond/light cream, granite/dark grey, or cobblestone (or a comparable color). Homes with different siding installed before July 1, 2014 are grandfathered in until it needs repair or replacement.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Exterior Paint and Siding →

Fencing & Yard Structures

What kind of fence is allowed, and the height and placement rules that go with it.

Can I install a fence? If so, what kind is allowed?

Only wood split-rail fencing is approved by the board (dark-painted wire or plastic mesh can be attached inside the rail). Fences installed before July 1, 2014 in a different style are grandfathered in until they need repair or replacement. All fencing and other exterior changes must be approved before work is started.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Fencing →
Are there height or placement rules for fences?

Fences and walls can’t exceed 48 inches above grade, unless a government safety code requires taller (like a pool enclosure). They also can’t be placed closer to the street than the midpoint line between the front corners of the home — except short decorative railings near an entrance, which can be up to 3 feet.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Fencing →

Pools & Recreation

In-ground pools, above-ground pools, hot tubs, and seasonal kiddie pools.

Can I install a swimming pool? If so, what kind?

In-ground pools are allowed once the HOA approves your design plans and all local permits are issued. Above-ground pools that extend 12 inches or more above grade are not permitted. Hot tubs, saunas, and temporary, seasonal children’s pools under 12 inches are fine — just remove seasonal kids’ pools by Labor Day each year.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Swimming Pools →

Signs & Yard Postings

Yard signs, real-estate signs, and what isn’t allowed.

Are there restrictions on yard signs?

Yes. No signs of any kind are allowed on a lot except: official street or identification signs installed by the Association, and one temporary real estate sign, no larger than six square feet, advertising that the home is for sale. Any other sign is a violation and can result in board action, including a fine.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Signs →
Can I put up a “for sale” or realtor sign?

Yes — you’re allowed one temporary real estate sign, no larger than six square feet, advertising that your home is for sale. It should come down once the home is no longer listed.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Signs →
What about security-company signs, political signs, or holiday signs?

Those aren’t among the exceptions in the covenants — only official street/identification signs and one real-estate-for-sale sign are permitted. Putting up any other sign, including security company, political, or holiday/seasonal signs, can result in board action, including a fine.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Signs →

Mailboxes

Keeping your mailbox and post to the neighborhood standard.

What are the requirements for my mailbox?

If your mailbox needs repair or replacement, it must be white, made of plastic or metal, and free from rust. It must be securely fastened to a weather-treated wooden post painted Sherwin Williams “Loggia” (color code SW7506) to match the neighborhood’s original design.

Source: HOA Policy Clarification — Mailbox →

Trash, Recycling & Garbage Cans

Pickup day, where cans can be stored, and the rules for screens and enclosures.

What day is trash and recycling picked up?

Garbage pickup is Monday morning — put your cans out Sunday night.

Source: New Resident Welcome Guide →
Where am I allowed to store my garbage cans?

Garbage cans must be concealed from public view using one of three options: inside your garage, up against the back of your house, or up against the side of your house with one approved screen or enclosure. Garbage cans are not allowed in the driveway or front of the house, except within 24 hours before or after your scheduled trash pickup. Any screen or enclosure needs Design Review Board approval before you install it.

Source: Garbage Can Storage Policy →
What are the rules for a garbage can screen or enclosure?

Screens must be the same color as your house or trim, black, or white, and built from wood, composite, or PVC. Height can’t exceed 50 inches and can’t extend more than 50 inches from your house, with a length no more than 12 inches past your last can (up to 4 cans). Boards or slats can’t have large gaps, the screen can’t be used for anything other than can storage, and it must be approved by the Design Review Board before installation.

Source: Garbage Can Storage Policy — Screen/Enclosure Regulations →
What happens if my garbage cans or screen aren’t in compliance?

The HOA will send a notification if your garbage placement or screen upkeep is out of compliance. A fine of $25 per month applies until it’s corrected, up to a maximum of $200 per year.

Source: Garbage Can Storage Policy — Violations →

Vehicles, Parking & Home Use

Parking rules, antennas and dishes, and running a business from home.

Can I park a truck, RV, boat, or trailer at my house?

A light pickup truck used as your personal vehicle is fine. But commercial vehicles, larger trucks, boats, trailers, campers, and mobile homes can’t be parked or stored on the street or on any lot — unless fully enclosed and shielded from view — for more than 48 hours within any 30-day period. Any structure built to enclose or shield a vehicle needs Design Review Board approval before it goes up.

Source: Covenants — Article IV, Vehicles →
Can I put up a satellite dish or antenna?

Exterior antennas and satellite dishes generally aren’t allowed, except satellite dishes under 24 inches in diameter, which are permitted if positioned to minimize visibility from the street.

Source: Covenants — Article IV, Antennae →
Can I run a business out of my home?

No business, trade, occupation, or profession may be conducted or established on your property without prior written approval from the board.

Source: Covenants — Article IV, Business →

HOA Board & Governance

How the board and committees work, meetings, voting, and which rules win in a conflict.

What does the HOA Board do?

The Board of Directors is elected by homeowners to two-year terms to make sure Riverwood Trails’ covenants are followed, dues are collected, bills are paid, and the HOA keeps proper funds in reserve. It’s a volunteer position.

What is the Design Review Board?

The Design Review Board is a separate, two-member board appointed by the Board of Directors that reviews and approves every application for exterior or grounds changes, keeping architectural standards consistent across the neighborhood. It’s also a volunteer position.

What is the Grounds Committee?

The Grounds Committee sources quotes for landscaping, lawn care, and general ground upkeep, and coordinates volunteers for grounds projects. Unlike the Board and Design Review Board, it’s open to any resident — no election required.

Who can serve on the Board, and how are trustees nominated?

The Board consists of three trustees, and each must be an owner (or the owner’s spouse) who occupies the lot — a person and their spouse can’t both serve at the same time. Nominees are put forward by a Nominating Committee: a board member serving as chair, plus two or more other association members, appointed at each annual meeting.

Source: Covenants — Code of Regulations, Board of Trustees →
How much notice do I get before an HOA meeting, and can I call a special meeting?

The Secretary mails or delivers notice of annual or special meetings to every owner between 10 and 60 days before the meeting. A special meeting can be called by the President, or by a written petition signed by at least 25% of the association’s total votes.

Source: Covenants — Code of Regulations, Meetings →
Can someone vote on my behalf if I can’t attend the annual meeting?

Yes. A vote allocated to a lot can be cast by a written, signed, and dated proxy. A proxy is only valid for one year from its date unless it specifies a shorter period, and you can revoke it in writing to the Secretary or the Association any time before it’s used.

Source: Covenants — Code of Regulations, Proxies →
If the covenants, articles of incorporation, and rules conflict, which one wins?

If Ohio law, the Declaration (Covenants), the Articles of Incorporation, and the Code of Regulations ever conflict, they’re applied in that order — Ohio law first, then the Declaration, then the Articles of Incorporation, then the Code of Regulations.

Source: Covenants — Code of Regulations, Conflicts →
